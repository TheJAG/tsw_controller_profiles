Safety systems · United Kingdom

British colour-light signals show how many sections ahead are clear, not the speed: green, double yellow, single yellow, red. Speed comes from the lineside speed signs and the route knowledge. Junction indicators and route indicators show a diverging route. AWS warns you at every caution, TPWS stops you at a red signal or an overspeed, the DSD / vigilance checks that you are awake. Quadrant controls are from TheJAG's UK profiles.

Safety systems

AWS
Automatic Warning System

A magnet 180 m before colour-light and distant signals and warning boards. Bell / chime = clear (green). Horn = caution, red or a speed warning: acknowledge within about 2.7 s or the brakes apply. The black and yellow sunflower then shows you did.

WhereEvery Network Rail route
QuadrantGear lever = AWS reset; left aux square = AWS (/ TPWS) isolation
TPWS
Train Protection and Warning System

Loops at red signals, speed restrictions and buffer stops. Train stop: passing a red signal brakes the train. Overspeed sensor: approaching too fast brakes it. Brake demand lamp flashes; the brakes release 60 s after the demand.

WhereEvery Network Rail route
QuadrantShares the AWS isolation (left aux square) on most units; Train Stop Override in the cab
DSD / vigilance
Driver's Safety Device, Driver Vigilance Device (DVD)

A pedal or handle held while driving; let go and the train brakes. Vigilance adds an alarm about every 60 s that you acknowledge.

WhereEvery modern unit and loco
QuadrantGear lever acknowledges in TSW (Q); right aux square = DSD / DVD isolation on most units
DRA
Driver's Reminder Appliance

A button you set when stopped at a red signal or a platform with the signal at danger: it cuts traction until you release it.

WhereUnits and locos that have one
QuadrantRound aux on the 385 and the 90; in the cab on the others
Tripcock
London Underground trainstops

A trainstop arm next to each LU signal is raised at red; passing it trips the tripcock on the train and brakes it.

Five white lights: the route diverges to the left (or right).

Speed signs and boards

Speed sign
Permissible speed from here, in mph (older lines: yellow cut-out figures).
DriveLower: by the sign. Higher: once the rear has passed.

A lower permissible speed ahead; AWS magnet 180 m before it.
DriveAWS horn: acknowledge, brake to the speed.

Temporary restriction ahead; AWS magnet. Two white discs (flashing white lights on lit boards).

How to drive under AWS, TPWS and the DSD

Approaching a caution

  1. Double yellow: AWS horn, press AWS reset (gear lever). Start braking.
  2. Single yellow: AWS horn again, reset. Brake to stop at the next signal.
  3. Keep below the TPWS trigger speed on the approach to the red; stop short.

At a red signal

  1. Stop before the signal. Set the DRA if you are waiting.
  2. Contact the signaller if the signal does not clear.
  3. To pass at danger with authority, press Train Stop Override (in the cab): it covers the train stop for about 20 s (60 s on the 66), never the overspeed sensor.
  4. Release the DRA when the signal clears, then take power.

TPWS intervention

  1. Brakes apply and the brake demand lamp flashes.
  2. Leave the controls; let the train stop.
  3. Acknowledge with AWS reset: the lamp goes steady. Report to the signaller; the brakes release 60 s after the demand.

Speed restrictions

  • PSR and TSR warning boards carry an AWS magnet: horn, reset, brake.
  • Be at the speed by the speed sign; TPWS overspeed sensors guard some restrictions.
  • Higher speed only once the rear of the train has passed the sign.

DSD and vigilance

  1. Keep the DSD / DVD switched in (right aux square: isolation off).
  2. When the vigilance buzzer sounds, acknowledge with the gear lever.
  3. Missed: the brakes apply; stop, acknowledge, release and drive on.

London Underground (1938, 1972 Stock)

  • Signals: red / green; a trainstop arm is raised at red.
  • Passing a red trips the tripcock: emergency brake.
  • A trip counts as a signal passed at danger: stop, report, then reset the tripcock (ENG 1) and the valve (gear lever, 1972) and continue slowly.
Before departure: AWS / TPWS and DSD isolation lights out: on most units the aux squares are isolation switches, unlit = in service (the 380 starts isolated). Flying Scotsman: left aux lit = AWS on; the LMS steam locos have no AWS. DRA released, the signal off.
